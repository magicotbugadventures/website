Designed by pediatric occupational therapists

The skills underneath handwriting, turned into play.

The Magic OT Bug is an iPad app of handwriting and fine motor games. Children trace, pinch, copy, steer and match their way through colorful games, and every game starts at level 1 and grows with them.

What your child practices

Five skill areas, designed by pediatric OTs

Activities designed by pediatric occupational therapists for practicing fine motor, handwriting, visual perception, coordination and following-directions skills.

  1. 01

    Handwriting and letter formation

    • Trace each letter in the right stroke order, watch it draw itself, then write it (Build & Write).
    • Color each letter by its size, tall, small or fall, on real writing lines (Color the Word).
    • Fix flipped and wrong-case letters, and hunt for the tricky b, d, p and q (Flip & Fix, Letter and Number Detective).
  2. 02

    Visual perception

    • Copy a design dot to dot, and rebuild black-and-white cube patterns (Copy the Design, Pattern Cubes).
    • Find where a row and a column meet, join edge-matching cards, and fit shapes into their holes (Criss Cross, Finish the Pattern, Shape Buddies).
    • Follow a caterpillar’s colors in order without lifting a finger (Caterpillar Munch).
  3. 03

    Coordination and motor planning

    • Trace two mirror-image butterfly wings at once, one hand on each (Butterfly Wings).
    • Race a friendly ghost around a giant figure eight to practice crossing the midline (Magic Loop Racing).
    • Tip the iPad in both hands to roll a ladybug home through a garden maze (Tilt Maze).
    • Follow a hopping frog with your eyes and tap it to clear the pond (Frog Pop).
  4. 04

    Fine motor

    • Pinch bugs between two fingers to build the pincer grasp behind holding a pencil (Pinch Pop).
    • Pop letter bubbles in A-to-Z order with one pointing finger (Bubble Train).
  5. 05

    Following directions

    • Read position clues and place pictures so every clue is true (Clue Crew).

Beyond the games

Magic Quests

Illustrated card adventures about everyday routines: getting ready for school, waking up and washing up, and a trip to town.

Made to feel good

Encouraging from the very first tap

  1. Spoken directions

    Each game shows its first move right on the board, and a gold “?” shows it again whenever it’s needed.

  2. Stickers to make pictures with

    Finished levels earn stickers for the Magic Sticker Book, where children make pictures of their own.

  3. Writing lines from your classroom

    Writing lines called “Tall · Small · Fall” or “Cloud · Flower · Ant”, to match your child’s classroom.

For grown-ups

For parents, therapists and teachers

The Grown-Up Portal

Behind a grown-ups-only check, the Grown-Up Portal shows what your child made and played this week, and their handwriting samples side by side over time. Add your own spelling words to the games that use them, and try any game yourself first without it counting toward your child’s progress.

2 Players and more children

With Magic Circle, two children can share the iPad and take turns on the same level, each finish saved to that child’s own record. Add more than one child to the iPad, and each keeps their own progress, stickers and handwriting samples.

Privacy

No ads. No tracking. No data collected.

Everything your child does stays on this iPad: no accounts, no chat, no third-party analytics. Purchases, links and sharing sit behind the grown-ups-only check, and Settings can erase all child data at any time.
